Option D · A chess clock pinned to the bottom

The top of the page stays as on main, even while you play: the timer, the guess box and the keyboard don't move down. The clocks sit in a dock at the bottom, where your thumb already is. The side that's ticking grows and fills in, so whose turn it is shows from across the table. This page walks through the whole duel on both phones: turning it on, joining, playing, the awkward moments and leaving.

What a "room" isOnly a shared name. Two phones that connect with the same name find each other. There are no accounts and no server of ours. That's why the app makes up a name nobody else would pick, and why the link carries it, so nobody has to type it.
How the phones find each otherBoth phones say "I'm in mint-owl-42" to a few public relays, swap a few KB of encrypted connection offers, then talk to each other directly. After that, each guess sends one tiny message. It's what clock.js does today, unchanged.
What's new in Option DOnly where the duel shows up: "How you play" in the drawer, a made-up room with a Share link, minutes as chips, the dock at the bottom and its sheet. The game itself, the scoring and the helpers stay as they are.

1Turning it on (player one)

The default game looks just as it does on main. The duel only exists once someone chooses it from the menu.

2Joining (player two)

Two ways in. The link is the easy one. Typing the room is the fallback when the two of you are sitting together and one reads it out.

3Connected

Once the phones find each other, the sheet folds away on its own. The dot in the dock's middle turns green, and both clocks wait at the full time until someone notes the first guess.

8 · The same moment on both phones

4Playing

Tapping a score (0–4) notes the guess, as on main, and in a duel it also passes the clock. There's no separate clock button to remember.

9 · Player one has just noted a guess

5Changing things mid-game

Everything about the duel is behind one button: the clock icon in the dock's middle. Anything that would restart a ticking clock asks first, as the code does today.

6Leaving

Leave duel in the sheet (view 14), or choosing Face to face in the menu, switches the duel off on that phone. The room is remembered, so it's one tap to set up again next time.

★Every state of the dock

The dock is the whole duel during play. Here are all its states side by side, so you can check each one reads clearly.

Waiting. Dimmed, hollow pulsing dot. You've connected, the other phone hasn't yet.

Ready. Green dot, both at the full time, nothing ticking.

Your turn. Your side is wide, filled and counting down.

Their turn. Theirs is. Noting a guess is what moves it.

Out of time. Red 0:00. Nothing ticks, and play can go on.

Dropped out. Hollow dot and a red line on the edge. The clock keeps going.

Other player left. Dimmed and stopped until they come back or you leave too.

Dark. In Advanced Helper Mode.

Trade-offs

  • + Nothing above the keyboard moves, in any mode. The game you know stays where it was.
  • + It reads like a real chess clock: big, at the bottom, and you can tell whose turn it is from across the table.
  • + Setting up is three taps for the first phone (logo, Duel clock, Connect) and two for the second (the link, then Join duel). Nobody has to invent a room name.
  • − The dock covers about 90px of the bottom, so the guess list and secret word need that much more scrolling.
  • − A fixed bar needs care with the iPhone home indicator and the phone's keyboard (view 10).
  • − New code beyond what's there now: the made-up room name, reading ?room= from the link, the "Join the duel?" sheet, the joining phone taking the other's minutes, and stopping the clocks when the other player leaves.
